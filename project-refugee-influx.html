Predicting Refugee Influx: The Role of Conflict Contagion and National Refugee Policy

We built a country-year panel (170 countries, 4,929 observations, 1992-2024) from UNHCR, UCDP conflict data, World Bank indicators and the Dataset of World Refugee and Asylum Policies (DWRAP), then predicted which countries see a surge in refugee inflows. Neighbor-aggregated features capture conflict and policy in bordering countries.

Method

  • Target: top-quartile year-over-year log change in refugees hosted
  • Temporal split: train on 1992-2019, evaluate on a 2020-2024 holdout
  • Four models: logistic regression, XGBoost, XGBoost with geography, XGBoost with policy

Findings

  • Holdout ROC-AUC rose from 0.793 (logistic regression) to 0.839 (XGBoost with the 12 policy indices)
  • No conflict feature reached the top ten by permutation importance, since lagged refugee counts already absorb recent conflict
  • The model under-predicts sudden shocks such as Syria 2015, Afghanistan 2021 and Ukraine 2022
